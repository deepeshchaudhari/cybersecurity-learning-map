# The Cybersecurity Landscape — Interactive Teaching Map
Published at [https://claude.ai/public/artifacts/00b63ddc-2409-47ee-91b2-95e75a1deb5d](https://claude.ai/public/artifacts/00b63ddc-2409-47ee-91b2-95e75a1deb5d)

An interactive, single-file visual map of the cybersecurity field, built for **teaching and learning**. It lays out **7 families** and **27 domains** of security as a branching map. Click any branch to open its domains, then pick a domain to watch a short animation explaining the core idea, alongside its key topics, common tools, and the career roles it leads to.

Everything lives in one self-contained `cybersecurity-map.html` — no build step, no dependencies, no internet required. Just open it.

---

## Why this exists

Newcomers often see "cybersecurity" as one thing. It isn't. This map helps students and self-learners see the whole terrain at once — how defensive, offensive, cloud, cryptography, governance, forensics, and emerging domains fit together — and gives each domain a quick, memorable visual so the concepts stick.

Great for classrooms, workshops, onboarding, career counselling, and anyone finding their way into the field.

---

## What's inside

- **Branching map** of 7 families → 27 domains, colour-coded per family.
- **A short animation for every domain** that illustrates the core mechanic — packets hitting a firewall, a coin hopping between wallets, an adversarial tweak flipping an ML prediction, a re-entrancy loop caught by an audit, and more.
- **Topics, tools, and career roles** for each domain, so learners know what to study and where it leads.
- **Search** across domains, topics, and tools to jump straight to any concept.
- **Projector-friendly and accessible** — legible on a screen, keyboard-navigable, and it respects reduced-motion settings.

The seven families: Defensive Security · Offensive Security · Application & Cloud · Cryptography & Identity · Governance, Risk & Compliance · Intelligence & Forensics · Specialized & Emerging.

---

## How to use it

**Just open the file.** Double-click `cybersecurity-map.html`, or drag it into any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari). It works fully offline.

**For teaching:** open a family branch, pick a domain, and let the animation loop on screen while you talk through it. The captions state each idea in plain terms, so it also works as self-study.

## A note on the content

The domain groupings aren't rigid silos — real work overlaps (DFIR touches incident response, Zero Trust shows up across several families). Tool lists are representative, not exhaustive, and the field moves fast, so treat them as "names you'll encounter," not a fixed syllabus. Corrections and updates via pull request are always welcome.
